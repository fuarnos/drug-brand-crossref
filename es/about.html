About

One job: turn a brand name into the actual substance, and say how sure we are. The site is public, ad-free and non-commercial. Every answer is a candidate set, never a prescription.

Three rules

  1. A brand name is not a primary key. Every query returns a set of candidates; identity is pinned by country × form × strength × active ingredient.
  2. Collisions are derived, not annotated. When one name hangs off different ingredients, the site flags “same name, different drug” automatically.
  3. No dosing advice. Identification and “not interchangeable” warnings only — no dose conversion, no substitution recommendations.

Nicknames and old names

“Two frogs” (liǎng zhī qīngwā) is not a brand name and is never stored as one: it points to an ingredient, metformin. Nicknames are a separate, hand-maintained table — searchable, but deliberately kept out of the brand-name tables. When one matches, the page labels it (internet nickname / older name) and then lists that ingredient’s brands; it is never presented as a name printed on a pack. Every entry carries a source; “user-provided” is shown as unverified and is not promoted to documented until an independent source is attached. No source, no new entry.

  • 57 nicknames are curated by hand in data/seed_nicknames.py and rebuilt — not scraped.
  • Nicknames never enter collision detection and never get a “same name” page: a nickname is not an identity, the ingredient is.

Folk names and class terms

A separate table (drug_term, 1,062 entries) holds folk names and category terms for legal medicines only: water pill, blood thinner, sleeping pill (shuìqiányào), the pill, morning-after pill, statin. Unlike nickname, it may point to a class rather than one ingredient, so substance_id is nullable; alias_all unions both tables for search. Drugs of abuse and recreational slang are explicitly out of scope (street / recreational drug slang); such entries were removed from the database and are not re-added.

  • NIDA / DEA lists are no longer used for recreational slang; only prescription-drug category terms that are legal medicines are kept, each with a source URL.
  • MedlinePlus (NLM) contributes common folk/category terms (US Government work); community sources (CN/GB/AU/ES) are labelled community name and stay searchable. FDA tall-man pairs: 22 official pairs. See the list.

Why “same name, different drug” happens

The same trademark is held by different companies in different markets, and the formula can differ completely. Verified examples: US Tylenol is single-ingredient acetaminophen 500 mg, while China 泰诺 is a four-ingredient cold medicine and the single-ingredient product is 泰诺林; US Benadryl is diphenhydramine, UK Benadryl is acrivastine; US Gaviscon is an aluminium/magnesium antacid, UK Gaviscon is an alginate raft.

Known gaps

  • China has 146,344 chemical-drug registrations, but ~9% resolve only to a China-only substance (no English INN yet), and China’s ATC is stored at level 4. Traditional Chinese medicines (TCM) are not loaded.
  • Strength equivalence stops at level D1 (same salt form); salt–base conversion (D2) and bioavailability judgement (D3) are not implemented.
  • Mexico has no strength column in the regulator’s lists; only 29% of products could be back-filled from public catalogues.
  • Canada’s DPD has no pack size or price, and many OTC consumer packs were authorised before the DPD’s electronic era, so a shelf product can be missing.
  • ATC coverage — two denominators: 47.6% of all substances (6,021 / 12,649) and 67.4% of drug entities (inn_drug, 5,702 / 8,459). The gap is mostly non-drug entities (homeopathic, herbal, food, material), not missing data. ATC data comes from Wikidata P267 (CC0) and ChEMBL (CC BY-SA 4.0); WHOCC’s ATC/DDD index needs a licence and is not used.
